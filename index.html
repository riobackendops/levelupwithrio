Process

Five steps. No mystery.

  1. 01

    Consult

    A free 15-minute call, or just chat on WhatsApp if that’s easier — I close deals over chat too. Tell me what you need; I’ll tell you if I’m the right fit, no prep required. If we’re not a match, I’ll point you somewhere better.

  2. 02

    Contract and deposit

    A simple contract, plain language, no legalese to decode. Payment upfront confirms your slot and your start date.

  3. 03

    Plan and assets

    You get an onboarding checklist: the logins, brand files, photos, and information I need to build. Everything in one list, sent upfront, so we’re not trading emails for a week.

    The 7-day clock starts when the checklist is complete, not when you sign. Send it all at once and your date holds.

  4. 04

    Go live

    Seven business days from the moment your checklist is complete. Website, landing page, launch, or a month of social content — it’s built, approved by you, and published.

  5. 05

    The support week

    Going live is where most people find the small things: a form that doesn’t send where it should, a button that reads wrong on mobile, a payment that needs testing with a real card. So the week after launch is on me, at no extra cost.

FAQ

Questions people ask before booking.

How soon can we start?

As soon as your onboarding checklist is complete. The seven business days are intensive and fully hands-on, so the clock starts when I have everything on the list: logins, brand files, photos, and the information about your business I need to write with.

I’m strict about this for one reason. A half-complete checklist stops the build dead. If I’m waiting on your logo files on day two, I can’t design around them, and the days lost waiting aren’t days I can make up at the other end — the week is already booked at full capacity. It’s the one part of the timeline I have no control over, and the only part that’s ever caused a delay.

So: gather it in one go, send it in one go, and your date holds. If something on the list doesn’t exist yet, tell me instead of leaving it blank and we’ll find a way around it.

Could something go live that I haven’t seen?

No. Nothing publishes without your approval. That’s the default and it doesn’t change unless you decide to change it. Some clients, after a few months of us landing in the same place every time, hand over the day-to-day approvals so they stop spending evenings reviewing captions. That’s your choice to make, whenever you want to make it, and never before.

How do I approve content?

Through your dashboard. You’ll get one at the start of the project, and everything moves through it: designs, drafts, and scheduled content, all in one place. No hunting through email threads for the latest version.

What if I want something changed?

Comment directly on the content in your dashboard during your review period, and I’ll make the updates within the revision window we agree on at the start — typically 24 to 48 hours.

What happens after the seven days?

You get a free support week. Live sites always surface small things that no amount of pre-launch checking catches: a form routing to the wrong inbox, a link that behaves differently on someone’s phone, a payment flow that needs testing with a real card. For seven days after go-live, minor fixes and small tweaks are included, and I’ll run the quality checks with you rather than leaving you to find things yourself.

After that, you own everything and it’s yours to run. For social, we continue month to month. Either way you’re not locked into anything long-term.

To be clear about the line: the support week covers fixes, adjustments, and things not working as they should. New pages, new features, or a change of direction are a new piece of work, and I’ll quote them properly rather than quietly absorbing them.

What if I don’t have brand assets yet?

Then we build them. A brand kit is included in every social plan, and website projects start with the visual direction if you don’t have one. You don’t need to arrive polished.

What I can’t make up is you. I’ll need real photos — of you, your team, your products, your space, whatever applies to your business — and real information about your offers, your customers, and how you talk about the work. These are on the onboarding checklist, and they’re the items that hold builds up most often, so it’s worth starting on them the day you book rather than the day before we start. Stock images and guesswork produce content that looks like everyone else’s, which defeats the point of hiring someone. If the photos don’t exist yet, I’ll tell you exactly what to shoot. A phone camera and decent light are usually all it takes.

Do you write the copy too?

Yes. Captions, page copy, and email sequences are part of the build. You’ll approve every word.

Who owns the files?

You do. Source files, logins, and assets are yours at the end of the project.

Do you work in my time zone?

I work with clients across the US, Europe, and Asia. Day-to-day runs on WhatsApp, Slack, or email, so time zones rarely matter. Live sessions are scheduled around yours.

Can I offer these services to my own clients?

Yes. I work with agencies, consultants, and community owners on a white-label basis: you sell it under your brand at your own prices, I build it behind the scenes. No hiring on your end, and nothing upfront, since your client pays you first. Book a consult and we’ll set up the terms.

How does payment work?

Payment upfront confirms your start date and reserves the build week. Longer launch projects can be split — we’ll cover it on the consult.
